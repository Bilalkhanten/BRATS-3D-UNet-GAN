@section[hard] Hard Synthetic Benchmark

@subsection[hd-design] Design of the benchmark

The phantom study of the previous section shows that the pipeline works but is saturated: all variants reach almost the same Dice score, so that it cannot rank them. To obtain a task on which the components of the model can actually differ, the phantom generator was given a second setting that makes the segmentation problem harder in the ways in which BraTS is hard (@tab:hd-gen). The lesions are smaller, so that an error of a few voxels matters; the enhancing shell is thinner, which makes ET small and difficult to separate from the core; the contrast between the tumour compartments and the normal tissue is lowered and the boundaries are blurred, which reproduces the gradual transitions of infiltrating glioma; and the noise and the multiplicative bias field are stronger. @fig:hd-examples compares one phantom of each setting. The hard benchmark is a synthetic task: it contains no anatomy, no artefacts and no inter-rater variability, and it is **not** evidence about the performance on patients. Its purpose is to exercise the complete analysis (ablation, paired statistics, sensitivity, seeds, folds, grading) on a problem that is not saturated, so that the analysis produces numbers that can be read, and so that published effect sizes can be compared with the variation measured here.

The experiments follow the plan of Chapter 3 in a reduced form that can be run on two CPU cores: the six cumulative variants A0 to A5 on fold 0 with two seeds (tiers A and C), two Tversky settings and four adversarial settings with one seed (tier B), and the variants A0 and A5 on the two other folds (tier D). All networks have 4 levels and 8 base channels (at most 64), are trained on $48^3$ patches with batch size 2 for 32 epochs of 50 iterations (the adversarial variants add 8 epochs of the adversarial stage; the other variants are trained for the same total number of generator updates), with AdamW at learning rate $10^{-3}$ in single precision. The discriminator has 8 base channels. Each run took 19 minutes on average. The unit of analysis is the phantom: where a variant has two seeds, the per-phantom metrics are averaged over the seeds before any statistic is computed.

@subsection[hd-tv] Tversky parameters and the recall--precision trade-off

Section @sec:losses derived that a Tversky loss with $\beta>\alpha$ weights false negatives more strongly than false positives and therefore shifts the operating point of the network towards higher sensitivity at the price of precision. The hard benchmark allows this prediction to be tested with three settings of $(\alpha,\beta)$ on top of the loss of A3, and compared with the loss without Tversky term. Adding the Tversky term with $(\alpha,\beta)=(0.3,0.7)$ to the loss of A3 changes the mean Dice by 0.018 [0.009, 0.028], the ET sensitivity by -0.038 [-0.100, 0.014] and the ET precision by 0.064 [0.041, 0.089] (paired bootstrap over phantoms). @tab:hd-tv and @fig:hd-tv give the Dice score, the precision and the sensitivity of the settings.

When $\beta$ increases from 0.5 to 0.8 the mean ET sensitivity changes from 0.718 to 0.589 (-0.129) and the mean ET precision from 0.295 to 0.387 (+0.091). This is the opposite of the shift predicted by the derivation of Section @sec:losses: the settings with a larger weight on false negatives are more conservative, not more permissive. The prediction concerns the gradient of a single loss term at a fixed network output, whereas the trained network also depends on the region-wise Dice and cross-entropy terms, on the deep-supervision weights and on the small number of phantoms; the result is reported as measured and is not used as support for the recall argument. The median ratio of predicted to reference ET volume lies between 1.78 and 2.27 for all settings, which shows a systematic over-segmentation of the enhancing tumour that is present for every loss, including the loss without Tversky term, and is therefore not caused by $\beta$.

@subsection[hd-grade] Grade classification from the predicted masks

The leak-free grading protocol of Section @sec:grade was run on the hard benchmark with the full model (A5, seed 1) trained on each of the three folds. With features of the predicted masks and logistic regression, the cross-validated AUC is 0.573 [0.467, 0.677] and the test AUC 0.922 [0.794, 1.000] (accuracy 0.812, sensitivity 0.792, specificity 0.875, expected calibration error 0.162); gradient boosting reaches a test AUC of 0.547 [0.309, 0.776]; with the features of the ground-truth masks the test AUC is 1.000 [1.000, 1.000], which is the upper bound for the segmentation quality of this benchmark. @tab:hd-grade-tab gives all four combinations of mask source and classifier. The grades of the phantoms differ by construction in the enhancement of the shell and the size of the core, so that the task measures how much of this information survives the segmentation error; the number of LGG-like test phantoms is small, and the intervals are accordingly wide. The cross-validated and the test AUC differ by 0.35, and their intervals do not overlap. Both estimates come from the same pipeline, so that the difference is a property of the data and cannot be attributed to a change of method: the cross-validated estimate rests on 128 development phantoms, the test estimate on 32 phantoms of which only a minority are LGG-like, and the AUC of such a small minority class is volatile. The experiment does not allow the discrepancy to be resolved; the test value is therefore to be read as an optimistic and unstable estimate, and the cross-validated value as the more reliable statement that the features of the predicted masks carry little information about the grade on this benchmark although the ground-truth masks carry all of it. Neither is a statement about BraTS.

Three features of the gallery deserve comment. First, the boundary of the whole tumour follows the reference closely in the figures, and the errors concentrate on the interface between the compartments and on the enhancing tumour, which is thin and low in contrast in the hard setting. Second, the failure cases of @fig:hd-g5 have the lowest Dice scores of the test set (0.592, 0.600) and are distinguished by the size of the reference regions (756, 833 voxels of whole tumour, against a median of 708) and not by an artefact of the benchmark. Third, the compartment that the networks disagree on is the enhancing tumour. On the two phantoms of @fig:hd-g6 the numbers of voxels labelled as enhancing are: phantom 019: reference 168, A0 336, A3 359, T0.8 241, A5 242; phantom 085: reference 165, A0 281, A3 281, T0.8 194, A5 193. A network that labels a multiple of the reference volume as enhancing, as the images of A0 and A3 show for these phantoms, makes the enhancing label cover the core and the shell together; this is the over-segmentation of the enhancing tumour that the volume ratios of @tab:hd-tv quantify over the whole test set. For the LGG-like phantoms of @fig:hd-g3 the reference contains 13, 0 enhancing voxels, and A5 labels 199, 197 voxels as enhancing; a detection of enhancement in a phantom whose reference has little or none is a false alarm of the kind that the empty-mask conventions of Chapter 3 are designed to penalise. The images are a selection and are not a statistical statement; the statistics are in the preceding subsections.

The fusion weights are uniform to within the precision of the table: the stem does not select among the modalities, neither on average nor between phantoms, and the weights of the two grades are indistinguishable. The weights are computed from globally pooled descriptors of stems that end in an instance normalisation, which standardises every channel of every volume and thereby removes most of the volume-specific information that the pooled descriptor could carry; this is a plausible reason for the uniform weights, which the present experiment does not isolate (it would require a variant without normalisation in the stems). As a consequence the stem behaves as a fixed averaging of four modality-specific branches followed by a mixing convolution, and a benefit of this component cannot be attributed to modality selection. This design limitation is discussed in Chapter 5.
