@section[phantom] Synthetic Phantom Study

The second kind of evidence is an end-to-end run of the complete training and evaluation pipeline on synthetic data with known ground truth. Its purpose is to establish that the pipeline, as implemented, learns, converges, evaluates, aggregates and tests as designed; it is not intended to rank the model variants for brain tumours (see the note in Section @sec:r-overview).

@subsection[ph-design] Design

One hundred phantoms of $48^3$ voxels were generated with the generator described in Appendix @app:phantom-gen: 75 are labelled HGG-like and 25 LGG-like, with an ellipsoidal brain with three tissue classes, four MR-like contrasts, a smooth multiplicative bias field, Rician-like noise, and a nested tumour (necrotic core, enhancing shell, oedema) written with the BraTS labels $\{1,2,4\}$. HGG-like phantoms have a strongly enhancing shell and a large core, LGG-like phantoms weak or absent enhancement. @fig:ph-examples shows two examples. The phantoms were split at the level of the phantom with the same code as for BraTS: a grade-stratified test set of 20 phantoms and three cross-validation folds on the remaining 80; only fold 0 was trained.

@fig ph-examples | file={ASSETFIG}/phantom_examples.png | width=6.2
Two synthetic phantoms (upper row: HGG-like, lower row: LGG-like). From left to right: T1, T1ce, T2, FLAIR, and the ground-truth labels.
@end

The network variants A0 to A5 are the cumulative steps of the ablation of Chapter 3 (A0, A1, A2, A3, A4, A5 available in this build), each built with 4 levels, 8 base channels (at most 64) and trained for 20 epochs of 50 iterations with batch size 2 on $48^3$ patches (A5 adds 8 adversarial epochs; the other variants are trained for the same total number of generator updates), AdamW with learning rate $10^{-3}$, on the CPU in single precision. The width and depth are therefore much smaller than the BraTS configuration of Chapter 3 (base width 32, depth 5), but the code path is the same. Each variant was trained with seed(s) 1, 2; where several seeds exist the per-phantom metrics are averaged over seeds before any statistic is computed, so that the unit of analysis remains the phantom.

@subsection[ph-res] Segmentation accuracy on the held-out phantoms

@tab:ph-main lists the accuracy on the 20 test phantoms. All variants reach a mean Dice above 0.996 for the average over the three regions; the largest difference between the means of any two variants is 0.0034 (A2 against A0), and the lowest Dice of any single test phantom and variant is 0.973. The task is therefore close to saturation: phantoms contain no anatomy, no artefacts and no label ambiguity, and the contrast between the tumour compartments is large compared with the noise. Under these conditions the pipeline can be shown to work, but it cannot discriminate between architectures, and the differences below must not be interpreted as evidence about the value of any component.

@subsection[ph-curves] Convergence

@fig:ph-curves shows that the training loss of the first run falls by a factor of 14 between the first and the last epoch and that the validation error falls from 0.44 at epoch 3 to 0.0047 at epoch 27 for A0. The final validation errors (mean over seeds) are A0: 0.0162, A1: 0.0001, A2: 0.0001, A3: 0.0005, A4: 0.0004, A5: 0.0006.

@subsection[v10] Diagnostics of the discriminator

@fig:ph-d shows the quantities that the training code records at every adversarial epoch for the 2 run(s) of the full model: the accuracy of the discriminator on real and generated label maps (fraction of patch-level outputs above or below 0.5), the two adversarial losses, and the weight $\lambda$ of the adversarial term, which rises linearly to 0.050 over 2 epochs.

@fig ph-d | file={FIGDIR}/ph_d.png | width=6.4
Discriminator diagnostics of the full model during the adversarial epochs: (a) accuracy on real and generated label maps, (b) discriminator loss and adversarial loss of the generator, (c) weight of the adversarial term. Line styles distinguish seeds.
@end

Averaged over the adversarial epochs, the discriminator classifies 88.1% of the real and 95.6% of the generated outputs correctly; at the last epoch the figures are 100.0% and 100.0%. The discriminator is therefore not trivially separating the two classes, which is the regime in which the adversarial gradient is informative; this is the behaviour that the label smoothing, the instance noise and the small weight of the adversarial term were designed to produce. The diagnostics are recorded for the BraTS runs in exactly the same way and are reported in Section @sec:brats-res; they should be read together with the Dice and HD95 differences, because a stable discriminator without an effect on the metrics is the expected outcome of a small adversarial weight.

@tab:ph-maf and @fig:ph-maf give the weights that the fusion stem assigns to the four modalities (variant A3). A uniform allocation corresponds to 1/4; the largest deviation from it, in the mean over the HGG-like test phantoms, is 0.004. The weights for HGG-like and LGG-like phantoms differ by at most 0.000, and their standard deviation across phantoms is at most 0.002.

The fusion weights are uniform to within the precision of the table: the stem does not select among the modalities, neither on average nor between phantoms, and the weights of the two grades are indistinguishable. The weights are computed from globally pooled descriptors of stems that end in an instance normalisation, which standardises every channel of every volume and thereby removes most of the volume-specific information that the pooled descriptor could carry; this is a plausible reason for the uniform weights, which the present experiment does not isolate (it would require a variant without normalisation in the stems). As a consequence the stem behaves as a fixed averaging of four modality-specific branches followed by a mixing convolution, and a benefit of this component cannot be attributed to modality selection. This design limitation is discussed in Chapter 5.

@subsection[ph-grade] Grade classification pipeline

The out-of-fold protocol of Section @sec:grade was run on the phantoms with the full model (A5, seed 1) trained on each of the three folds. @tab:ph-grade-tab gives the result: with the features of the predicted masks and logistic regression, the test AUC is 1.000 [1.000, 1.000] and the cross-validated AUC 1.000 [1.000, 1.000]; with the ground-truth masks the test AUC is 1.000 [1.000, 1.000]. The phantom grades differ by construction in the enhancement of the shell, so a high AUC is expected and shows that the feature extraction, the leak-free fold assignment and the classifier code work end to end; with only 20 test phantoms, of which about five are LGG-like, the intervals are wide and the number carries no information about the achievable AUC on BraTS.
