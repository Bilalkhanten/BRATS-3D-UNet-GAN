@chapter[disc] Discussion

@section[d-intro] Purpose and Structure of the Chapter

This chapter interprets the evidence of Chapter 4. Because the BraTS experiments are executed separately from the preparation of the text (Section @sec:status), the chapter is organised so that it remains valid whatever the data show: it states in advance what each possible outcome of each hypothesis would mean, and it separates the conclusions that the existing evidence already supports from those that depend on the BraTS runs. Section @sec:d-frame gives this interpretation framework, Section @sec:d-verif discusses what the verification experiments establish, Section @sec:d-hard reads the evidence of the hard synthetic benchmark, Section @sec:d-prelim relates the protocol to the numbers of the preliminary study, Section @sec:d-threats lists the threats to validity, and Sections @sec:d-limits and @sec:d-ethics treat the limitations, the clinical context and the ethical aspects.

@section[d-frame] Interpreting the Hypotheses

Each hypothesis of @tab:hyp is a statement about a paired difference on held-out patients, and each is decided by the rule given there. A decision rule alone, however, does not say what the outcome means for the design of a segmentation system. @tab:interp therefore records, before the data are seen, the reading of the three possible outcomes (the interval of the difference lies on the hypothesised side of zero, includes zero, or lies on the opposite side).

| ID | Supported | Interval includes zero | Opposite direction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| H1 | The component adds measurable accuracy in this configuration and is retained | Accuracy is unchanged within the resolution of the design (@tab:mde); the component may still be justified by robustness or efficiency, but not by Dice | The component harms in this configuration; the interaction with the other components or the training budget has to be examined before it is recommended |
| H2 | The asymmetry shifts the operating point towards sensitivity as derived in Section @sec:losses; the precision cost has been measured | The loss weights have little effect once the hybrid loss and the sigmoid threshold are in place | The prediction of the derivation fails; the threshold of 0.5 or the BCE term dominates |
| H3 | The adversarial term acts as a boundary regulariser | The adversarial term is neutral; its cost in training time is not justified | The adversarial term degrades boundaries, which is the typical sign of an unstable game (Section @sec:v10) |
| H4 | Predicted segmentations retain enough information for grading | Grading from predicted masks is not reliably better than the threshold of 0.80 with 74 test patients | Segmentation errors remove the signal that the ground-truth masks carry |
| H5 | The proposed model matches the strong reference within the margin | The comparison is inconclusive: the interval is too wide or the difference too close to the margin | The reference is better by more than the margin; the added components do not compensate for the missing self-configuration |
@end

Two features of the design affect all readings. First, the margin of H5 and the minimum detectable differences of Section @sec:v6s are small compared with the variation between folds (@tab:hd-cv): a difference of 0.005 Dice units can be statistically clear in the paired analysis of 74 patients and still be smaller than the change that another fold or another seed would produce. Differences of that size are reported with their intervals but are not interpreted as improvements of the method. Second, the ablation is run on a single partition and a single seed per variant, so that an interval excludes zero only with respect to the sampling of the test patients (Section @sec:exp-design); a stronger claim would require repeated trainings, which the confirmatory use of H1 would need if its outcome were close to the decision boundary.

@section[d-verif] What the Verification Experiments Establish

The analytical and numerical verification of Section @sec:verif supports the following statements, each of which is reproduced by an automated script of the code base and none of which depends on a trained model.

*The overlap losses are far less sensitive to class imbalance than the cross-entropy.* At a tumour fraction of 0.2% the tumour receives a few per cent of the gradient mass under the cross-entropy and a large fraction of it under the Dice and Tversky losses (@tab:v2), as the analysis of Section @sec:losses predicts. The consequence for the design is the hybrid objective: the overlap term guarantees a usable gradient when the lesion is small, and the cross-entropy supplies a bounded and well-conditioned gradient when the predicted overlap is already high.

*The Tversky weights act as a controlled bias.* The ratio of the penalties for a missed and a spurious voxel tends to $\beta/\alpha$ (@fig:v9), which makes the sensitivity--precision trade-off of H2 a quantitative prediction rather than a tuning accident. The prediction concerns the loss, not the final classifier: after thresholding at 0.5 the realised trade-off also depends on the calibration of the probabilities, which is why H2 is tested on the measured sensitivity and precision and not inferred from the loss.

*The cost of the architectural components is known exactly.* The attention gates are inexpensive in parameters and computation, whereas the fusion stem and the residual blocks increase the memory of the activations substantially (@tab:cx). A practitioner with a fixed memory budget therefore pays for these two components in patch size or batch size, which is a confounder of the ablation if the memory is the limiting resource; the experimental design controls it by keeping the patch size and batch size equal for all variants, and mixed precision is used because the full model needs about 1.8 times the activation memory of the plain network (@tab:cx).

*The test set is large enough for the planned comparisons, but not for subgroup claims.* The bootstrap interval has nominal coverage at the size of the test set and under-covers below about forty patients (@tab:cov), and the minimum detectable paired difference is about 0.016 Dice units at a standard deviation of the differences of 0.05 (@tab:mde). Statements about the 15 LGG patients of the test set are therefore descriptive.

*The slice, not the patient, is not an independent unit.* The adjacent-slice correlation and the design effect of Section @sec:v5s show that thousands of slices carry the information of hundreds of independent observations on the phantoms; for real tumours the effect is at least as large. This is the empirical justification of the patient-level partition, and it explains why slice-level splits, which are common in the literature, can produce optimistic scores through leakage between neighbouring slices of the same patient.

The *phantom study* supports a narrower conclusion. It shows that the full pipeline (data conversion, patient-level split, training of all variants, evaluation with the metric conventions, aggregation, the leak-free grade protocol, and the diagnostics of the adversarial game) runs end to end and produces internally consistent output. It does not support conclusions about the relative merit of the components, for two reasons discussed in Section @sec:ph-res: the task is saturated (Dice above 0.99 for all variants), and the number of phantoms is small. A difference that is statistically clear in this setting reflects the training budget and the phantom population, and it should not be transferred to brain tumours. The study is nevertheless useful as a regression test: a defect in the sampling, the loss, the optimiser or the evaluation would have shown as a failure to converge or as an inconsistent metric table.

@section[d-prelim] Relation to the Numbers of the Preliminary Study

The preliminary study reported a Dice of 0.99 and an IoU of 0.97 [@prelim]. Three observations concern their interpretation; none of them is a statement about the quality of the preliminary work, whose aim was different, but all are needed to decide what can be compared with the results of this thesis.

*The reported pair is internally inconsistent as a pair of means.* By the bound of @eq:dice-iou, a mean Dice of 0.99 requires a mean IoU of at least 0.980 (Section @sec:v1-s), and a mean IoU of 0.97 allows a mean Dice of at most 0.985. The pair can arise only if the two numbers were computed in different ways (for example, one over voxels pooled across cases and the other averaged over cases, or on different subsets). The protocol of this thesis computes every metric from one confusion matrix per patient and region and aggregates by the mean over patients, so that the bound is satisfied by construction and can be checked on every table.

*The task definitions differ.* The preliminary study segmented volumes that were cropped around the tumour and measured one aggregate overlap; this thesis segments the whole brain and measures three nested regions. For a given classifier, the Dice of a region with $T$ true-positive, $F$ false-positive and $M$ missed voxels is $2T/(2T+F+M)$, so that a crop that removes distant background removes false positives that a whole-brain evaluation would count. The gain is largest for small regions (ET, TC), which are precisely those with the lowest published Dice values. A comparison of the two settings would therefore have to be made on the same inputs, which the code base supports (it can evaluate any model on cropped or whole volumes) but which is not a hypothesis of this thesis.

*The published challenge values are not comparable either.* The numbers quoted in Section @sec:bg-data were obtained by the organisers on a hidden test set, which is not accessible; the training release used here has different statistics, and a five-fold cross-validation on 295 patients plus a 74-patient test set is not the challenge protocol. The reference of the thesis is therefore the nnU-Net baseline trained on the identical partition, not the published numbers.

@section[d-threats] Threats to Validity

*Internal validity.* (i) *Single seed.* Each BraTS configuration is trained once. The differences between variants combine the effect of the component with the variation due to initialisation and patch sampling; the phantom study shows the order of magnitude of this variation for a small problem, and the cross-validation exposes the variation between data partitions. (ii) *Equal training budget.* All variants receive the same number of generator updates (87,500), but the adversarial variant spends additional computation on the discriminator, so that the comparison is equal in generator updates and not in wall-clock time. (iii) *Hyper-parameters.* The configuration of every variant uses the defaults of Chapter 3; no hyper-parameter search was made on the test set and none on the validation sets except the choice of the checkpoint. A variant may be disadvantaged if its optimum differs from the default, and this applies in particular to the adversarial weight, whose sensitivity is probed by four runs (Section @sec:hd-gan). (iv) *Model selection.* The checkpoint with the best validation Dice is chosen; the same validation data are used for the out-of-fold analyses, which gives them a small optimistic bias that the test set does not have. (v) *Software defects.* The test suite and the phantom study reduce, but cannot eliminate, the risk of defects; the scripts and configurations are released with the thesis so that an independent run can detect them.

*Construct validity.* The Dice coefficient and the 95th percentile Hausdorff distance are the standard metrics of the benchmark, but neither captures the clinical utility of a segmentation: a small displacement of the boundary of a large tumour changes the Dice little and may change a surgical plan, while a missed small enhancing focus changes the Dice of ET a great deal and may be clinically irrelevant [@metricsreloaded]. The empty-region conventions (Dice 1 when both masks are empty, 0 when only one is) matter for the LGG patients, many of which have no enhancing tumour; they are fixed in advance and stated, but another convention would change the mean ET Dice. HD95 is reported in millimetres on a one-millimetre grid; it is not symmetric in the sense of a directed distance, and it is sensitive to isolated false-positive voxels, which the absence of post-processing leaves untouched.

*External validity.* BraTS volumes are co-registered, skull-stripped and resampled by the organisers; they come from a limited number of institutions and scanners and from patients before treatment. The results cannot be assumed to transfer to unprocessed clinical scans, to post-operative imaging, to other pathologies or to other scanners. The grading task inherits the label noise of the release, whose grades are provided by the organisers under the classification in force at the time of the annotation (Section @sec:bg), which has since changed [@who2021]. The test set of 74 patients contains 15 LGG patients; any claim about LGG performance is correspondingly imprecise.

*Statistical conclusion validity.* The hypotheses are specified before the experiments, the family of H1 is corrected for multiplicity, and the effect sizes and intervals are reported next to the *p*-values. Nevertheless, the number of secondary comparisons (the three regions, two metrics and several variants) is large; those that are not part of H1 to H5 are exploratory and are labelled as such. The nnU-Net comparison inherits all the above and, in addition, depends on the version and the default configuration of the baseline software, which is stated with the results.

@section[d-limits] Limitations

The limitations that follow from the construction of this thesis, and that a reader should keep in mind independently of the numbers, are the following.

- *No BraTS result in the preparation environment.* The environment in which this document was produced has neither a graphics processor nor access to the BraTS data. The BraTS section of Chapter 4 is therefore generated from output files that the experiments write on the machine of the author, and its status is stated in the section itself.
- *Single dataset and single task definition.* The study uses the training release of BraTS 2020 with the label convention of that year. The labels of later releases differ (a larger cohort from 2021 on) and the model has not been evaluated on them [@brats21].
- *No uncertainty estimate for single predictions.* The framework outputs region probabilities but does not calibrate them or provide per-patient uncertainty; the calibration of the grade classifier is reported, and that of the segmentation probabilities only for the hard synthetic benchmark (Section @sec:hd-calib).
- *No post-processing.* Removing small connected components or relabelling enhancing tumour that is smaller than a threshold is common in challenge pipelines and typically improves the Dice and HD95 of ET for patients without enhancement. It was omitted so that the reported numbers measure the model and not the post-processing; it can be added without retraining.
- *Computation.* The experiments are limited to $128^3$ patches, one seed, and a training budget of 350 epochs; longer training, test-time augmentation and ensembling, which the baseline applies at inference by default (mirroring), can change the ranking.
- *Clinical use.* Nothing in the thesis supports clinical use of the models. A deployment would require a prospective validation, a regulatory process, monitoring for distribution shift and a defined workflow in which a radiologist remains responsible for the segmentation.

@section[d-design] Design Alternatives Considered

Several alternatives were considered and not adopted; the reasons are recorded because they are part of the evidence on which the design rests. *Transformer encoders* [@unetr; @swinunetr; @transbts] were not adopted for the primary model because the aim is to study components that can be added to a convolutional network at a controlled cost, and because the evidence that they surpass a well-configured convolutional network on BraTS is mixed; they would be a natural extension of the framework, since the discriminator and the loss are independent of the generator. *Self-configuration* of the patch size, spacing and normalisation, as done by nnU-Net, was not integrated, so that the comparison with nnU-Net also measures the value of this configuration; this is a deliberate choice to keep the code base compact and the ablation interpretable. *Wasserstein and gradient-penalty objectives* [@wgan; @wgangp] were not adopted, since the least-squares objective with spectral normalisation is the simpler choice for a conditional patch discriminator with a $70$-voxel receptive field; the hinge alternative is included in the sensitivity runs. *Hard thresholds on the region probabilities with a connected-component filter* were rejected for the reason stated above. *Slice-based (2D) or tri-planar models* were rejected because the three-dimensional context of the lesion is the main source of information for the separation of the necrotic core from the enhancing rim, and because the 2D alternative would complicate the leakage control.

@section[d-ethics] Ethical and Regulatory Considerations

The BraTS data are publicly released, de-identified and distributed under the terms of the challenge and of The Cancer Imaging Archive [@tcia]; the thesis uses them under those terms and does not attempt re-identification. No new patient data were collected, and the synthetic phantoms contain no patient information. Three further points deserve mention. First, *fairness and representativeness*: the cohort is drawn from a limited set of institutions, and the performance in groups that are under-represented in it (for example, other scanner vendors, paediatric patients, or different ethnic groups) is unknown; any extension to a clinical setting would have to examine subgroup performance explicitly. Second, *automation bias*: a segmentation that looks plausible can be accepted without review, which is a recognised risk of decision-support software; the tests of this thesis do not address it. Third, *energy use*: the full experimental programme (Section @sec:exp-design) requires many GPU-hours, which the RUNBOOK states as an estimate so that the cost can be weighed against the information gained; the staged tiers allow the reader to stop after the first without loss of the central comparison.

@section[d-summary] Summary of the Chapter

The interpretation framework of @tab:interp fixes in advance the meaning of every outcome of H1 to H5. The verification experiments establish properties of the losses, the architecture and the statistical procedure that the BraTS experiments rely on, and they show that the pipeline is free of gross defects; they do not rank the components. The numbers of the preliminary study cannot be compared with those of this thesis because of the inconsistent Dice and IoU pair, the cropped inputs and the different task definition. The threats to validity (single seed, default hyper-parameters, one data release, label noise, small subgroups) bound the strength of any claim that the BraTS results will support.
