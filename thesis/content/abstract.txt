Accurate delineation of glioma sub-regions in multimodal magnetic resonance imaging is a prerequisite for quantitative treatment planning, but published deep-learning results are difficult to compare because of cropped inputs, case-level or slice-level data leakage, unreported metric conventions and the absence of controlled ablations. This thesis extends a preliminary study of a residual attention U-Net with adversarial refinement into a fully specified three-dimensional framework for the segmentation of whole tumour, tumour core and enhancing tumour and for the classification of low-grade versus high-grade glioma, evaluated on the BraTS 2020 training data under a leak-free protocol.

The framework combines a five-level residual 3D U-Net with additive attention gates on the skip connections, a modality-attentive fusion stem, deep supervision, region-based outputs, a hybrid Dice, Tversky and cross-entropy loss, and a conditional 3D PatchGAN with least-squares objective, spectral normalisation, label smoothing and instance noise. The thesis derives the gradient behaviour of the overlap losses under class imbalance, the relation between Dice and IoU with its feasibility bounds, the gradient of the attention gate, the properties of the fusion stem, the optimal discriminator and the trivial-separation pitfall, the receptive field, and the exact parameter and operation counts of every variant. It specifies a patient-level, grade-stratified partition with an automatic leakage check, whole-volume sliding-window inference, per-region Dice and 95th-percentile Hausdorff distance with stated conventions for empty masks, bootstrap intervals, paired tests with multiplicity correction, a non-inferiority margin against nnU-Net, and five hypotheses with decision rules fixed before the experiments.

Numerical verification confirms the derived properties: at a tumour fraction of 0.2% the tumour receives 2.7% of the gradient mass under the cross-entropy but 43% under the Dice loss; the Dice and IoU values reported in the preliminary study cannot be means over the same cases; and the planned test set of 74 patients can detect paired differences of about 0.016 Dice units.
